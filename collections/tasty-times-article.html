Do Monster Energy Drinks Actually Work?

By Connor Deneau · November 19, 2019

By now you have likely heard of Monster energy drinks. Along with Red Bull, they are one of the most prominent energy drink brands in the world, and they sponsor hundreds of athletes across action sports.

With that much success signing professional athletes, you would think drinking Monster helps athletic performance. We went looking for the answer.

What is actually in a Monster

Monster does not make its ingredient list easy to find. Here is what a 16 ounce can contains, and what each item is doing.

  • Caffeine, at 160mg per 16 ounce can
  • Sugar, and a lot of it
  • Artificial sweeteners in the zero sugar versions

The caffeine question

Not all caffeine is the same. Some is naturally derived from plants and some is produced synthetically. Synthetic caffeine is the cheaper option and the one most large energy drinks reach for.

Clean caffeine gives you the lift without the crash on the other side.

So does it work?

It will wake you up. Whether that is the kind of energy you want is a different question, and it is the reason we built Kill Cliff the way we did.
